# Official judging rubric (reference)

Rating scale: 1 Poor · 2 Below Expectations · 3 Meets Expectations · 4 Good · 5 Excellent

## Principles

- Fairness across disciplines — ML not mandatory; relevance over complexity
- Evidence-based — demonstrated functionality only
- Hackathon scope — focused functional MVP > incomplete ambition
- Responsible AI — understand, validate, disclose AI use

## Tie-breaking (first difference wins)

1. Number of event tools used (more is better)
2. Relevance and Readiness to Deploy
3. Validation, Testing, and Evidence
4. Software Engineering and Technical Execution
5. Proof of Demand and Practical Impact
6. Judges' collective real-world value

## 1. Problem Understanding and Relevance — 15

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Clear definition of the problem and its target users | 5 |
| Relevance of the solution to the identified problem | 5 |
| Understanding of the problem's causes, constraints, and context | 5 |

Ask: Is the problem clear? Does the solution address it? Context understood? Appropriate for users?

## 2. Data Quality, Analysis, and Signal Extraction — 10

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Appropriate data sources and understanding of the data | 3 |
| Data cleaning, preparation, and handling of data quality issues | 2 |
| Meaningful analysis, patterns, insights, or feature selection | 3 |
| Appropriate interpretation of variables and findings | 2 |

SE teams: DB design, data structures, API integration, use of available data.  
ML not mandatory.

## 3. Software Engineering and Technical Execution — 25

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Functionality and completeness of the working prototype | 9 |
| Appropriate architecture, implementation, and technology choices | 6 |
| Code quality, modularity, and maintainability | 5 |
| User experience, usability, and interface design | 5 |

Simple reliable effective > complex broken.

## 4. Validation, Testing, and Evidence — 15

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Appropriate testing of functionality and system behavior | 4 |
| Evidence supporting the solution's effectiveness | 5 |
| Appropriate evaluation metrics and interpretation of results | 4 |
| Identification of limitations, assumptions, and failure cases | 2 |

Evidence: tests, metrics, demos, before/after, edge cases.

## 5. Proof of Demand and Practical Impact — 10

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Evidence of user needs or demand for the solution | 4 |
| Potential to improve an existing process or solve the problem | 3 |
| Feasibility, scalability, and sustainability | 3 |

## 6. Relevance and Readiness to Deploy — 15

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Usable today by a real user, without the team's help | 5 |
| Deployed or deployable: live link, setup steps, configuration handled | 4 |
| Reliable on the main path: errors handled, no crashes, sensible data | 3 |
| Relevant now, with a small and clearly named list of what's left to polish | 3 |

Judge what exists now. Tools alone don't score here; readiness does.

## 7. Presentation, Communication, and Transparency — 10

| Sub-criterion | Pts |
|---------------|-----|
| Clear explanation of the problem and proposed solution | 3 |
| Effective demonstration of the working prototype | 3 |
| Clear explanation of technical decisions and results | 2 |
| Transparency regarding limitations, assumptions, and AI usage | 2 |
